Compiled 2026-07-29 while implementing the documentary requirements module.

HOW TO READ THIS FILE
---------------------
The specification documents are the authority. The code is an implementation of
them and carries no authority of its own, so a difference between the code and
the documents is never a choice to be made — it is either work still to do, or
a defect. Only the documents can conflict with each other, and only those
conflicts need a decision.

The file is therefore in four parts:

  PART 1  Conflicts between the documents          NEEDS YOUR DECISION
  PART 2  Documented, not yet built                no decision — work to do
  PART 3  Documented, blocked by the platform      needs an exception
  PART 4  Built without a document saying so       confirm or remove

Every claim carries a page or file:line reference so it can be checked rather
than taken on trust.

PART 1 — CONFLICTS BETWEEN THE DOCUMENTS  (needs your decision)
================================================================================

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
C1. When does the unit go On Hold?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[DB]   Reservation Terms 1-2:
         "The reservation application shall be processed only after the
          reservation fee has been verified by the Billing Department."
         "Once payment has been verified, the selected unit shall be placed
          under On Hold status while the reservation is being evaluated."
[SUM]  p.2 — "After saving the reservation, the system automatically changes
       the unit status to On Hold to prevent double selling."  Immediately,
       with no mention of payment verification.

The documents describe two different systems.

  Hold on submission        protects against double-selling, which [MS] p.21
                            calls the elimination of "double reservations or
                            double sales"; but a buyer who never pays can hold
                            a unit indefinitely.

  Hold after fee verified   matches the terms the buyer legally agrees to and
                            the real business process; but leaves a window in
                            which two buyers can both submit for the same unit
                            — the exact problem the study set out to solve.

This is the most consequential conflict in the specification. Whichever way it
is settled, the losing wording has to change: the reservation terms are shown
to the buyer verbatim, so the system must not promise a sequence it does not
follow.

QUESTION Q1.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
C2. Is "BIR Form No. 1904 / TIN" required, or situational?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[DB]   Step 8 lists it among the documents to be uploaded, and — unlike the
       Marriage Certificate and the Special Power of Attorney — without the
       qualifier "(if applicable)". That reads as required.
[DB]   Step 1 (buyer information) appears to print a TIN field without the
       asterisk that marks every required field on that step. That reads as
       optional.

[DB] contradicts itself. Note the caveat above: Step 1 could not be read
cleanly from the binary file, so this should be confirmed in Word before it is
settled.

QUESTION Q2.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
C3. Is "Reservation Form" a document the buyer uploads?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[SUM]  p.2 lists "Reservation Forms" among the uploads, in both the online and
       the walk-in flow.
[DB]   Step 8 lists the documents to be uploaded, and Reservation Form is not
       among them.

The two are reconcilable, and the distinction matters. In [DB] the reservation
form IS the online application — Steps 1 to 10 are the form — so there is no
separate file. In [SUM] the walk-in buyer fills in a paper form which the
employee then scans, so it exists as a file only for walk-ins.

If [SUM] is followed for online buyers, the company must publish a downloadable
form. A requirement to upload a document the company never issues cannot be
met by anyone.

QUESTION Q3.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
C4. On approval, does the unit become Reserved or Sold?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[MS]   p.21 — "Once the reservation has been reviewed and officially approved
       by authorized personnel, the property's status automatically changes to
       Sold."
[MS]   p.13 — places the sale much later: the Contract to Sell is prepared
       after the first amortisation, and the transaction concludes with the
       Deed of Absolute Sale.
[SUM]  p.3 — "On Hold ↓ Reserved / Sold", offering both without distinguishing.

[MS] disagrees with itself: p.21 marks the unit Sold at reservation approval,
while p.13 places the sale several stages later. An approved reservation is
not a completed sale, so calling it Sold would misstate the company's position
in its own records.

QUESTION Q4.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
C5. How many projects, and which unit types?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[DB]   Project dropdown names five: The Legaspi Place, Summit Residences,
       Parkview Heights, Harbor View Residences, Greenfield Gardens.
       Unit Type dropdown names six: Studio, Studio Deluxe, One Bedroom,
       Two Bedroom, Three Bedroom, Penthouse.
[Verbal] the developer stated three projects in total.
[Floor plans] THE_LEGASPI_PLACE/ contains four unit types — Studio, 1BR, 2BR,
       3BR — with real areas, unit counts and floor ranges. No Studio Deluxe
       and no Penthouse.

[DB] and the developer disagree on the project count. And the six unit types in
[DB] may be a generic dropdown rather than a statement about these buildings —
the only floor plan sheets that exist show four.

PART 2 — DOCUMENTED, NOT YET BUILT  (no decision — work to do)
================================================================================

These are in the documents and absent from the code. None is a choice; they are
listed so that none is mistaken for one, and so the remaining scope is visible.

W1. RESERVATION FEE AMOUNT AND PAYMENT DETAILS
    [DB] Step 4 has a Reservation Fee field and Term 1 makes processing depend
    on its verification, but the portal never states an amount, a payee or a
    channel. The buyer is asked to upload proof of a payment nobody has told
    them how to make. Together with C3 this is the same failure twice:
    requirements that cannot be satisfied from what the portal provides.

W2. PURCHASE PRICE BREAKDOWN
    [DB] Steps 2-3: Price per Square Metre, Unit Purchase Price, Parking
    Purchase Price, Total Purchase Price, several marked "system generated".
    A unit currently carries a single flat price.

W3. PAYMENT TERMS AND SUMMARY
    [DB] Steps 4-5: down payment percentage (10-50%), promotional discount,
    down payment installment term (Spot Cash through 36 months), financing
    option (Bank / PAG-IBIG / Cash), and a computed payment summary.

W4. PARKING SPACE
    [DB] Step 2: optional parking with slot number, type (Regular / Premium /
    Tandem), area and price.

W5. THIRTY-DAY DOCUMENT DEADLINE
    [DB] Term 3 and [SUM] p.4: documents within 30 calendar days of the
    reservation date, after which the employee cancels. The wording is shown
    to the buyer but nothing computes or stores a due date, and no overdue
    reservation is surfaced to staff. Displayed, not enforced.

W6. RESERVATION REFERENCE FORMAT
    [DB] System Confirmation shows "RES-2026-000001". The code shows the first
    eight characters of the Firestore auto-ID. A sequential reference needs a
    counter document and a transaction to increment it safely.

W7. MANAGEMENT APPROVAL FOR CANCELLATION
    [SUM] p.4 — cancellation is "manually cancelled by the employee, subject to
    management approval". Staff can cancel; there is no second approval step,
    and no MANAGEMENT role exists in the role model.

W8. BILLING, PAYMENT MONITORING, LOAN MONITORING
    [MS] modules 6, 7 and 8 (p.32) and [SUM] p.4: Statement of Account,
    balances, payment history, penalties, association dues, and Bank/PAG-IBIG
    loan tracking. Substantial modules. Whether they belong to Phase 1 is
    QUESTION Q6 — that part is a decision; building them is not.


================================================================================
PART 3 — DOCUMENTED, BLOCKED BY THE PLATFORM  (needs an exception)
================================================================================

These are the only places where the code cannot simply follow the documents.
Each needs either a paid plan or an accepted exception.

B1. EMAIL AND SMS NOTIFICATIONS
    [MS] p.15 — "receive transaction notifications".
    [DB] — "You will receive a notification once your reservation has been
    reviewed."
    Firebase cannot send mail from client code. This needs Cloud Functions,
    which require the Blaze (paid) plan, or a third-party mail service with
    its own key and cost. The same constraint already forced the workaround in
    authService.ts, where staff accounts are created through a secondary app
    instead of the Admin SDK.
    Currently the portal shows status in-app only, and nothing is sent.
    NEEDS: a paid plan, or acceptance that notifications are in-app only.

B2. CAPTCHA / ABUSE PROTECTION
    [DB] Registration includes "I am not a robot".
    Firestore security rules cannot express a rate limit — they constrain what
    may be written, never how often. The standard remedy is App Check with
    reCAPTCHA, which is free to configure but must be enabled on the Firebase
    project and wired into both apps.
    Every write now requires an authenticated account, which removes the
    anonymous flooding path (see SETUP.md), but an authenticated user is still
    unthrottled.
    NEEDS: App Check enabled on the project, or acceptance of the risk for a
    defended prototype.


================================================================================
PART 4 — BUILT WITHOUT A DOCUMENT SAYING SO  (confirm or remove)
================================================================================

Behaviour no document specifies. Each was added because a documented goal
required it, but none of them is authorised by the specification, so each is
listed for confirmation. If any is unwanted, say so and it comes out.

X1. ID SUBTYPE VALIDATION (Stage 1b)
    [MS] p.21 requires detecting "Birth Certificate selected, Driver's License
    uploaded". It says nothing about one ID versus another. But a single
    Valid ID category treats every government ID alike, so a buyer selecting
    Driver's License could upload a PhilHealth card and pass. [DB] Step 8's
    "Choose ID type" control implies the finer check, without describing it.

X2. FRONT AND BACK OF THE ID
    No document asks for both sides. Philippine IDs carry material data on the
    reverse — restrictions on a licence, address on a PhilSys card — which
    staff cannot verify unseen. The front is validated; the back is only
    checked for being a genuinely different image, because the reverse has no
    issuing header to match against.

X3. THE ID IS VALIDATED BEFORE THE UNIT IS HELD
    No document specifies the ordering. OCR runs in the browser, so the ID can
    be read and matched with no upload at all. Checking first means a buyer
    with the wrong document never takes a unit off the market and no orphaned
    file is left in storage. Consistent with [DB] Step 8, which collects the
    ID during the application.
    NOTE: this interacts with C1. If the hold moves after payment
    verification, this ordering changes with it.

X4. BUYER-INITIATED CANCELLATION
    [SUM] p.4 describes cancellation as an employee action. Buyers can now
    withdraw their own reservation while it is pending or under review, which
    releases the unit. [DB] Term 5 names "voluntary withdrawal of the
    reservation application" as a legitimate cause, and a unit held by a buyer
    who has moved on is a unit the company cannot sell. Staff cancellation
    exists alongside it.

X5. STAGE 2 IS ADVISORY, NOT BLOCKING
    [MS] p.21 says a type mismatch is rejected outright, and it is. No document
    says what happens when the NAME does not match. It is surfaced to the buyer
    and to staff but does not block, because OCR misreads names routinely —
    the manuscript's own example is CRVZ for CRUZ — and blocking would refuse
    legitimate buyers over photo quality. Consistent with [MS] p.57: OCR
    "should not be viewed as a complete replacement for human document
    verification".
    QUESTION Q7 — this one is worth confirming explicitly.

R1. MAXIMUM UPLOAD SIZE  (resolved 2026-07-29)
    [MS] p.57 states only "a maximum file size specified by the company" — no
    figure. [DB] Steps 7 and 8 say 10 MB, twice. [DB] is the company doing the
    specifying, so the documents never actually disagreed.
    The code had 3 MB, with a comment attributing it to the manuscript's Scope
    and Limitation. The manuscript contains no such figure; the attribution was
    simply wrong. The practical cost was real — a phone photo of an ID is
    routinely 4-8 MB, so the system refused documents the specification
    promises to accept, before OCR ever ran.
    Changed to 10 MB in SFSR-Shared/src/constants.ts to match [DB].
